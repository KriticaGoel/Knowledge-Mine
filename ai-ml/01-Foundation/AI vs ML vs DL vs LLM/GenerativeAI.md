They are of two type of models:
* Generative AI
* Discriminative AI.

**Generative AI** refers to models that can generate new data instances that resemble the training data. 
These models learn the underlying distribution of the data and can create new samples, such as images, text, or audio. 
Examples of generative AI include Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), 
and Transformer-based models like GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer).

**Discriminative AI**, focuses on distinguishing between different classes or categories of data. 
These models learn to map input data to specific labels or outputs, making them suitable for classification tasks. 
Discriminative models do not generate new data but rather predict the probability of a given input belonging 
to a particular class. 
Examples of discriminative AI include Support Vector Machines (SVMs), Logistic Regression, and 
Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) used for image classification.

Generative AI has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to create realistic and high-quality content. It has applications in various fields, including art, music, natural language processing, and even drug discovery. For instance, GANs have been used to generate photorealistic images of people who do not exist, while VAEs have been employed to create new molecular structures for pharmaceutical research.

Discriminative AI, while not as flashy as generative models, plays a crucial role in many practical applications. It is widely used in tasks such as spam detection, sentiment analysis, and medical diagnosis. Discriminative models are often preferred when the goal is to make accurate predictions based on existing data rather than creating new data.

In summary, both generative and discriminative AI have their unique strengths and applications. 
Generative AI excels in creativity and content generation, 
while discriminative AI is essential for classification and prediction tasks. 
Understanding the differences between these two types of AI can help researchers and practitioners 
choose the right approach for their specific needs, 
whether it be generating new data or making accurate predictions based on existing information.
